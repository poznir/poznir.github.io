1. What the Application is

The Application is an educational tool. It guides a person, step by step and by voice, through head-positioning exercises — the Dix-Hallpike test and the Epley maneuver — that clinicians use for benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV), and uses the phone's motion sensors to indicate whether the head appears to be in the position the instructions describe. Its side check only records which positions you yourself report as causing spinning.

The Application does not diagnose any condition, does not provide treatment, and is not a substitute for medical assessment. It is not a medical device and has not been evaluated or approved by any health authority. It is intended for adults whose doctor or physiotherapist has recommended these exercises to them.

2. Medical disclaimer

This app is an educational tool for guiding positions and does not constitute medical advice or formal treatment. In any case, this is for educational purposes only, and everyone who uses it does so entirely at their own risk.

The Application is not for emergencies. If your symptoms are sudden or severe, call your local emergency services.

Do not use the Application, and see a doctor first, if any of the following apply to you:

  • A new or unusually severe headache with the dizziness
  • Double vision, or trouble seeing to one side
  • Slurred speech, or trouble finding words
  • Weakness or numbness in the face, arm, or leg
  • Trouble walking or standing that persists between attacks
  • New hearing loss or ringing in one ear
  • Dizziness that is constant, rather than triggered by position changes
  • Dizziness after a recent head or neck injury
  • Severe or unusual neck pain or stiffness with the dizziness
  • Fainting, chest pain, or palpitations with the dizziness
  • Neck pain, stiffness, or a neck problem — surgery, fusion, disc or instability issues
  • Back pain or a back problem that makes lying flat, or turning in bed, difficult
  • Severe rheumatoid arthritis or cervical spondylosis
  • Osteoporosis of the spine, or a recent fracture of the spine, neck, or hip
  • Known carotid or vertebral artery disease, or a recent stroke or mini-stroke
  • Uncontrolled high blood pressure, or a heart condition that limits exertion
  • A detached retina, or recent eye surgery
  • A recent fall, or difficulty getting up from lying down without help
  • Under 18 years of age
  • And any other condition, injury, limitation, or circumstance — physical or mental, listed here or not — that could make lying back, rolling over, or turning your head unsafe or difficult for you. If you are not sure, do not do it; ask your doctor first.

Pregnancy. The Application was not designed for use during pregnancy, at any stage. Lying back flat and quick head and body movements can be uncomfortable or unsafe. Ask your doctor or midwife before using it, and have someone with you the first time. If you continue, you do so at your own risk and on the advice of your own doctor or midwife; to the fullest extent permitted by law, the Service Provider accepts no liability for any outcome of use during pregnancy.

Using it safely. Have someone nearby the first few times. Use a bed with clear space around it — not a sofa edge, and nothing hard to hit; the phone covers your eyes during a sequence. Move slowly; if you feel very unwell, sit up carefully and stop. Stop and seek medical care if you get a new severe headache, vision or speech changes, weakness or numbness, chest pain, or fainting.

Sensor feedback can be wrong. The position feedback depends on your phone's sensors and on how you hold the phone, and can be inaccurate. It never confirms that a position is correct or safe for you, and you must not rely on it as such.
